# Private Party RV Purchase Action Plan

VILIGANS COMMAND CORPORATION

Prepared: August 2, 2026

Status: Draft executive, legal, finance, and operations work product. This file does not authorize an RV purchase, deposit, financing application, title transfer, insurance bind, lease, pledge, grant representation, or passenger-service launch.

## Executive Summary

The Private Party RV Purchase project can support VCC only if the RV is clearly classified before purchase. The two viable use cases are:

1. Mobile outreach, intake, partner events, grant-readiness demonstrations, and community mobility support.
2. A non-passenger auxiliary operations asset supporting the Wyoming rural transportation pilot.

The RV should not be treated as the primary special-needs or NEMT passenger vehicle unless counsel, WYDOT, the insurer, and operations confirm that the vehicle is legally and practically suitable for the exact passenger service model. The current priority remains a wheelchair-accessible van or approved partner vehicle for passenger transportation.

Recommended position: keep the RV purchase on a controlled diligence track while prioritizing paid pilot revenue, insurance, operating authority, accessible vehicle readiness, and grant/partner support.

## Legal Risks

| Risk | Level | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Misclassifying RV as transportation vehicle | Critical | Separate mobile outreach use from passenger transportation unless WYDOT, insurer, and counsel approve the specific model. |
| Private-party title defects | High | Require clean title, lien release, VIN/brand check, odometer statement, and seller authority before any money changes hands. |
| Undisclosed defects | High | Use independent inspection; make purchase contingent on inspection and insurability. |
| Related-party or insider transaction | High | Require conflict disclosure, board approval, fair-value support, and written terms. |
| Grant-funded asset mismatch | High | Do not represent the RV as grant-eligible capital unless grant rules permit the use, title holder, procurement method, useful-life controls, and match treatment. |
| Personal-use contamination | Medium | Adopt no-personal-use policy, mileage log, storage plan, maintenance log, and asset ledger entry. |
| Warranty/as-is exposure | Medium | Use written purchase terms; disclose no implied operational readiness; reserve repair budget. |

## Risk Assessment

Overall risk rating: High until use classification, title, insurance, inspection, financing, ownership, and Wyoming operating-use analysis are complete.

The highest-risk path is using the RV for paid passenger transportation or implying NEMT readiness before WYDOT, insurance, ADA/accessibility, vehicle suitability, and driver controls are approved. The lower-risk path is treating the RV as a mobile outreach and operations-support asset while the passenger pilot relies on an accessible van or approved partner vehicle.

## Compliance Requirements

- Written use-classification memo before purchase.
- Clean title, lien release, VIN/brand check, odometer, bill of sale, and seller authority file.
- Independent inspection and repair estimate before price approval.
- Insurance bindability confirmation for the exact use, owner, driver, storage, and event profile.
- Board/officer approval before deposit, purchase, financing, title transfer, or related-party lease.
- WYDOT operating authority and USDOT/FMCSA applicability memo before compensated passenger use.
- HIPAA/BAA and Workspace sharing-control review before collecting rider medical, Medicaid, disability, or trip-purpose health details.
- Asset ledger, no-personal-use policy, mileage log, maintenance log, and storage plan after closing.

## Financing Options

| Option | Best Use | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Cash purchase with board cap | Lowest closing complexity if cash runway remains adequate. | Do not drain launch reserve needed for insurance, authority, driver, and accessible vehicle readiness. |
| Credit-union or bank vehicle/RV loan | Useful if price and condition support collateral value. | Lender may require personal guarantee, full coverage, title lien, and proof of business use. |
| Seller financing | Can reduce immediate cash need. | Requires strong contract, lien/title handling, default terms, and board approval. |
| Equipment loan | Possible if RV is treated as business equipment/outreach asset. | Underwriting may reject older RVs or require high down payment. |
| Lease or short-term rental alternative | Tests mobile outreach value before purchase. | May be better than buying until revenue and use case are proven. |
| Sponsor-funded asset support | Aligns with community outreach and brand visibility. | Sponsorship must avoid control promises, unapproved advertising obligations, or restricted-use conflicts. |

## Funding Implications

The RV can help funding only if it strengthens partner outreach, sponsor visibility, mobility-needs evidence, or coordinated access narratives without consuming launch cash needed for the core passenger pilot. It should not replace the accessible vehicle budget unless the RV is specifically approved as suitable, insurable, compliant, and operationally practical for the intended rider profile.

Do not include the RV in a grant budget until the applicant path, procurement rules, title restrictions, match treatment, useful-life controls, and eligible-use language are confirmed in writing.

## Asset Acquisition Strategy

Ranked strategy:

1. Preserve launch cash and accessible passenger-vehicle priority.
2. Treat RV as a mobile outreach or operations asset unless specifically approved for passenger use.
3. Require clean title, lien release, VIN/brand check, seller authority, and written purchase terms.
4. Make any offer contingent on inspection, insurance bindability, board approval, and final use classification.
5. Compare acquisition cost with lease/rental and accessible van alternatives.
6. Add RV to the corporate asset ledger only after title, registration, insurance, and storage plan are complete.
7. Maintain separate mileage, maintenance, event-use, and repair logs.

## Corporate Ownership Considerations

Preferred default: title the asset in the entity that owns and controls the approved use, subject to counsel, lender, insurer, tax, and grant restrictions.

Controls required before execution:

- Board consent approving purchase or financing.
- Officer authority and signer resolution.
- Conflict and related-party disclosure if seller, lender, storage provider, or user is related to an officer/shareholder.
- Asset-use policy distinguishing mobile outreach from passenger service.
- No-personal-use policy.
- Insurance naming the correct owner, operator, lienholder, and additional insureds as required.
- Tax and depreciation review by CPA.
- Grant-title restrictions review before using grant or match funds.

## Insurance Requirements

Request broker review for:

- Commercial auto or business-use RV coverage.
- Physical damage, comprehensive, collision, and stated/actual cash value treatment.
- General liability for mobile outreach events.
- Hired and non-owned auto if the RV is not titled directly to the operating entity.
- Inland marine or equipment coverage for onboard technology, supplies, and accessibility equipment.
- Cyber/privacy if intake or trip information is collected from the RV.
- Workers compensation and volunteer coverage implications.
- Abuse/molestation coverage if vulnerable populations are served at outreach events.
- Umbrella/excess liability.

Insurance gate: no deposit, pickup, event use, transportation use, or financing close until the broker confirms bindability for the exact use and ownership structure.

| Medicaid/NEMT | Medicaid billing should not be assumed for RV-based service. | Verify Medicaid provider and vehicle requirements before any Medicaid-related representation. |
| Records/privacy | Intake in or around an RV can create sensitive data. | Use Workspace HIPAA/BAA and sharing-control gate before PHI or trip-purpose medical details are stored. |

## Operational Impact

Positive impact: the RV could create a visible mobile outreach platform for rural partner events, senior/disability intake days, veteran mobility support, sponsor activations, and demand mapping.

Negative impact: an RV purchase can slow launch if it consumes cash, requires repair work, creates insurance complications, distracts from accessible van acquisition, or creates public confusion about passenger-service readiness.

## Business Risks

1. RV consumes cash needed for insurance, authority, accessible vehicle, driver, and launch reserve.
2. RV is not accepted by insurer or regulator for intended use.
3. Purchase price exceeds fair market value after repairs.
4. Repairs delay pilot launch.
5. Grant reviewers reject the RV as ineligible or low-priority capital.
6. Seller title/lien defect blocks registration.
7. Public outreach implies services are ready before launch gates are complete.
8. Personal use or undocumented use undermines corporate asset controls.

## Required Board Decision

Board/officer approval should answer:

1. What is the maximum all-in acquisition budget?
2. Is the RV approved only for outreach/operations, or also for any transportation use?
3. Which entity will own the RV?
4. What financing, if any, is authorized?
5. What insurance must be confirmed before closing?
6. Who may sign purchase, financing, insurance, title, and storage documents?
7. What conditions must be satisfied before closing?

## Recommended Actions And Next Steps

1. Keep the RV project in diligence status until the use-classification memo is approved.
2. Collect seller, title, lien, VIN, odometer, inspection, repair, and insurance evidence.
3. Run a side-by-side financial comparison: RV purchase, RV rental, accessible van lease, accessible van purchase, and partner-vehicle agreement.
4. Prepare a board approval package with price ceiling, financing ceiling, signer authority, ownership entity, insurance conditions, storage plan, and no-personal-use rule.
